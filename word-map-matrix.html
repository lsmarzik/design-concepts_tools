1. Build Your Word Map

Start with one central subject, then explore it through several different categories.

What is a word map?
A word map helps you move beyond the first, most obvious ideas. Place your main subject in the center, then create categories that help you look at that subject from different directions. Under each category, add specific words that could eventually become something visual or symbolic.
  • Things you can see: objects, tools, shapes, places, materials, animals, structures, natural forms
  • Actions or movement: pull, split, grow, connect, drift, stack, fold, circle
  • Feelings or qualities: trust, tension, calm, energy, mystery, protection — especially ideas that could be expressed symbolically
  • Associations: words, metaphors, memories, rituals, environments, or ideas connected to the subject
The goal is to collect visual associations that can later be combined into concepts. You are not designing the logo yet.

2. Explore the Word Matrix

Combine two ideas to discover a third meaning that could become a logo concept.

Think like a logo designer.
The goal is not simply to draw two things next to each other. Look for a way to combine two ordinary ideas into one new symbol. Ask yourself:
  • Could these two ideas become one visual?
  • What new meaning would appear if they were combined?
  • Could one idea become the shape, negative space, texture, movement, or structure of the other?
  • Would the result communicate something about the business without illustrating it literally?
Two ideas + one visual relationship = a third meaning.
